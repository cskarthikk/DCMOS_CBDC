CBDC-PUF: A Novel Physical Unclonable Function Design
Framework Utilizing Configurable Butterfly Delay Chain
Against Modeling Attack
YANJIANG LIU, JUNWEI LI, TONGZHOU QU, and ZIBIN DAI, Information Engineering
University
Physical unclonable function (PUF) is a promising security-based primitive, which provides an extremely
large number of responses for key generation and authentication applications. Various PUFs have been de-
veloped as central building blocks in cryptographic protocols and security architectures, however, the existing
PUFs and their improvements are still vulnerable to modeling attacks (MA) with refined machine learning
algorithms. In this article, a configurable butterfly delay chain-based PUF design framework is proposed to
meet the requirements of randomness, reliability, uniqueness, and MA-resistance metrics. A configurable
butterfly delay chain is introduced to create multiple pairs of symmetric paths and a strong PUF relying on
the intrinsic delay fluctuations of two identical paths is built. Furthermore, a secure hash function is used
to insert non-linearities into the PUF, and a BCH-based error correction algorithm is utilized to recover the
actual responses under noisy environments. The proposed PUF is implemented on Xilinx FPGAs and three
machine learning algorithms are used to evaluate the resistance against MA. Experimental results show that
the randomness, reliability, and uniqueness of the proposed PUF are close to the ideal value (49.6%, 99.9%,
and 49.9%, respectively), and the prediction accuracy reaches 50% that indicating a desirable resilient to MA.

make them difficult to be deployed in cost-sensitive systems. A promising approach to protect

Since the Arbiter-based PUF first presented by Lim et al. [2], PUF has attracted widely attention
in the field of hardware security. After that, numerous PUFs, including the Arbiter-based PUF [3, 4],
RO-based PUF [5–7], SRAM-based PUF [8, 9], Latch-based PUF [10, 11], Flash-based PUF [12, 13],

solutions for cryptographic products [14, 15]. Furthermore, several PUF-based security solutions,
like the device identification and authentication [16], secure storage of cryptographic secret [17],
IP protection [18], anti-counterfeiting [19], and so on, are already explored in the literature and
integrated into the high-security products that circulate in the market. Among all existing PUFs,
the butterfly PUFs achieve high reliability, randomness, and uniqueness, which have recently re-
ceived increasing attention from both academics and the industry [20–22]. Therefore, the butterfly
PUFs have become the most appealing option for establishing trust in resource-constrained em-
bedded systems. However, the original butterfly PUF is a weak PUF and has a limited number of
challenge–response pairs. In this case, the butterfly PUF is only used as the secret key, which does
not scale to the authentication and attestation fields.
PUFs are based on the intrinsic physical differences caused by the random manufacturing pro-
cess which are infeasible to predict and reproduce in practice. However, the PUF behavior with
those differences is easily modeled by machine learning algorithms and the PUF’s response of
an arbitrary challenge can be predicted accurately by this mathematical model. Several works de-
scribed in [3], [23], and [24] perform a successful modeling attack (MA) on the existing PUFs.
Furthermore, several MA resistant PUFs, including the XOR Arbiter-based Physical Unclon-
able Functions (APUFs) [3, 25, 26], feedforward APUFs [2, 27, 28], lightweight secure PUFs [29],
and so on, have been explored to resist the modeling attacks. Although several studies enhance the
resistance against MA, they are still vulnerable under refined machine learning algorithms with
enhanced power consumption and area cost. Moreover, PUFs are susceptible to the varying supply
voltage and ambient temperature variations, and PUFs provide slightly different responses when
queried with the same challenge multiple times, which cannot be used directly as cryptographic
keys. In summary, the existing PUFs suffer from one or more of the following disadvantages:
(1) they are vulnerable to modeling attacks with refined machine learning algorithms, (2) they are
subject to varying operating conditions, and (3) they introduce non-negligible power consumption
and area overhead to enhance the resistance against MA.
In this article, we design a high-security and low-overhead butterfly PUF to process security-
critical and privacy-sensitive data. A path-configurable delay chain is designed to generate
multiple combinations of two symmetrical paths, a cross-coupled circuit with two delay chains is
constructed to produce two possible stable states and a flip-flop-based arbiter is used to settle to
one of the two stable states. Then, the configurable delay chain of arbiter PUF and the nonlinear
mathematical model of butterfly PUF are combined to form a Configurable Butterfly Delay
Chain-based Physical Unclonable Function (CBDC-PUF), which produces a large number of
challenge/response pairs (CRPs) and achieves a high MA-resistance ability. Furthermore, an
MA-resilience solution based on the secure hash algorithm is introduced to break the linear
relationship between the challenges and responses, which makes the conventional machine

BACKGROUND
2.1
Attack Model
Recently, various third-party EDA tool vendors, Intellectual Property providers, and foundries
from different countries and regions are participating in the PUF design and fabrication process.
We assume that the total PUF design and fabrication stage are trusted, and its toolchain is reliable.
Therefore, the attack scenario is mainly at the application stage, and adversaries can access the
PUF-based chips but have little knowledge about the PUFs. Numerous attack approaches, including
micro probing attack, side-channel attack, fault injection attack, MA, and so on, are exploited by
adversaries to retrieve the PUF responses in this stage. The actual PUF does not require several
specific memory cells to store the responses and cannot be accessed through the communication
interfaces. To evaluate the performance of PUF effectively, we suppose that some CRPs can be
collected freely via interfaces.
2.2
Physical Unclonable Functions
Considering the security requirements of embedded systems, numerous PUFs have been proposed
over the past several decades. It can be broadly classified into (1) non-electronic PUFs, (2) analog
electronic PUFs, (3) delay-based PUFs, and (4) memory-based PUFs. Among all the existing PUF
instantiations, delay- and memory-based PUFs are the most appealing ones for the embedded
systems that are used in critical applications and sensitive fields. From [30], arbiter PUF is first
presented by Lim et al. and its efficacy is evaluated on the application-specific integrated circuits.
However, its linear additive path delays are also vulnerable to MA. To further improve the MA-
resistance ability, a feed-forward arbiter PUF is proposed to use the intermediate value of arbiters
as the parts of the challenge [2]. But References [31] and [32] have successfully implemented
the MA on the feed-forward arbiter PUF. Majzoobi et al. demonstrate that advanced modeling
techniques can predict the response of the feed-forward arbiter PUF [5, 33]. After that, Pranesh
et al. introduce an XOR APUF design scheme [23], Rashid et al. present a reconfigurable arbiter PUF
[3], Nimesh et al. design a hybrid nonlinear PUF [24], and Durga et al. propose a multiplexer-based
composition of arbiter PUF design approach [4] to thwart MA. Among them, XOR APUF is the most
efficient approach to resist MA. Although XOR arbiter PUFs improve the computational complexity

Ring oscillator PUF (RO-PUF) is another type of delay-based PUF, which exploit the dif-

oscillator is susceptible to the fluctuations of voltage and ambient temperature, and the reliability
of basic RO-PUF is relatively low. To improve the reliability of RO-PUF, numerous improved RO-

offset-based RO-PUF [38, 39], and so on, have been developed to reduce the bit error rate of re-
sponses. Besides, References [6, 7, 40, 41] increase the number of CRPs of traditional RO-PUF.
However, the tradeoff between the implementation cost, reliability, and MA-resistance is still the
main challenge of the existing RO-PUFs.
Due to the existence of manufacturing process variations, the physical properties vary with
each other. Memory cells have two possible stable states (“0” and “1”), the stable state a cell enters
depends on the physical properties of its transistors. It is extremely difficult to guess the stable
state of a specific cell. Memory-based PUFs exploit the instability behavior of the memory cells
to produce physical unclonable and unpredictable responses. Since the SRAM-PUF first presented
in [8], various memory cells, including the latches [10, 11], Flash [12, 13], D flip-flops [42, 43],
and scan flip-flops [44–46], have been explored to design the memory-based PUF. Generally, the
memory-based PUF is a weak PUF and the CRPs space size depends on the number of memory cells.
The larger size the CRP space is, the more cells the memory-based PUF needs, and the larger the
area occupies. Moreover, majority of memory-based PUFs require the power-up behavior to settle
to a stable state from two possible states. There is no doubt that such memory-based PUFs need a
dedicated power network to implement multiple power-up operations. In addition, the stable state
stored in the memory cells can be revealed by various physical attacks (e.g., micro probing attack
[47], side-channel attack [48], and fault injection attack [49, 50]), which makes the memory-based
PUFs susceptible to physical attacks.

The hash function is a mathematical function that converts an arbitrary length of an input
value into a fixed length, which is widely applied to password storage, data integrity check, and

EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND ANALYSES
4.1
Experiment Setup
For a CBDC-PUF, the width of the control signal is set as 16-bit. There exist 216 pairs of symmet-
ric paths for a CBDC-PUF and thus the number of CRPs is 65536. A total of 128 CBDC-PUFs are
arranged in a matrix and thus the CRPs space of the PUF framework is 65536128. Furthermore, a
secure hash algorithm-3 (SHA-3) is used to generate 2048-bit hash data that configures 65536128
pairs of symmetric paths of the PUF framework. To improve the robustness of the proposed PUF,
several primitives of FPGA, including the LUT2, NAND2, and FDCE_1, are used to build the struc-
ture of CBDC-PUF. Besides, the placement of CBDC-PUF is adjusted and fixed using the FPGA
Editor, and the routing of two differential signal paths is modified to be approximately equal. The
fixed structure of CBDC-PUF is saved as a hard macro and reused to form a 128-bit PUF response.
The proposed PUF occupies 256 NAND2s, 8192 LUT2s, and 128 FDCE_1s, while the total design
requires 4406 registers, 9544 LUTs, 4568 slices, 4 bonded IOBs, and 1 BUFG. The utilization of the
number of slice registers is 3%, the utilization of the number of slice LUTs is 15%, the utilization of
the bonded IOBs is 1%, and the utilization of the number of the BUFG is 3%. Moreover, we compare
the FPGA utilization results of the proposed design with the several works (FFXOR [32] and 4-XOR
APUF [23]) in Table 1. We use the Xilinx XPower Analyzer to calculate the power consumption of
total design. The dynamic supply power is 0.427 W, and the quiescent supply power is 0.089 W, and
the total supply power is 0.516 W. We also calculate the computation latency of the total design,
and the computation latency is 10.627ns and the maximum frequency is 92.799 MHz.

In the actual situation, PUFs are subject to variations of environmental conditions, and the re-
sponses have several error bits under the same challenge. However, the PUF-based application
scenarios require an error-free response as the authentication password or cryptographic key. So,
an error correction code should be considered to correct several bit errors from the PUF response.
Considering the hardware complexity and error-correcting capability, an error correction algo-
rithm based on the BCH code is adopted and used to solve the bit-error problem of PUF response.
In this article, the BCH code is characterized as (31, 16, 3) and 3-bit errors can be corrected from
the 16-bit response.
To evaluate the performance of the proposed PUF, an FPGA-based PUF test platform is built
to acquire the CRPs under varying test conditions. The total project of the PUF framework runs
on the RISC-V evaluation board (Nuclei MCU200T) [55], and the corresponding FPGA is Xilinx
XC7A200T-2FGG484. A serial communication interface establishes a communication link between
the PC and FPGA-based platform. Besides, an automatic PUF response acquisition tool based on
the LabVIEW is built to obtain responses with the same challenge multiple times and analyze its
performance results (e.g., robustness, uniqueness, and randomness).
4.2
Performance Analysis
Process variations, including inter-die variations and intra-die variations, are the sources of PUFs.
In this article, we use an FPGA to validate the efficacy of PUF and only exploit the intra-die varia-
tions to generate the PUF response. We change the location of the occupied FPGA primitives and
design 10 macros to emulate 10 PUF implementations. Randomness, stability, and uniqueness are
the main performance metrics of the PUF, and the detailed calculation methods are presented in
[56]. A total of 50000 random challenges are generated and applied to the proposed PUF. Figure 3
shows the randomness results of 50000 responses. As shown in Figure 3, the randomness ranges
from 27.3% to 61.7% and more than 81.9 percent of responses fall within the range of 40% to 60%. For
the randomness quantification approaches, the much closer to 50% the results are, the more uni-
formly the logic “0” and logic “1” distribute, and the better the randomness of results is. To ensure
the unpredictability characteristic of the PUF, the randomness results of 11202 CRPs range from
48.4% to 51.6%, which are chosen and used as the candidate for security fields. Figure 4 shows the
distribution of logic “0” and “1” of those responses. Where the black and white filled box represent

observed that the logic “0” and logic “1” are uniformly distributed. More specifically, the averaged

5
CONCLUSION
In this article, a CBDC-PUF is proposed, which exploits the intrinsic delay differences between two
symmetrical paths to produce a large set of high responses. Furthermore, a cryptographic hash
function is presented to obfuscate the linear relationship between the challenges and responses,
which improves the resistance against MA. Moreover, a bit-instability correction mechanism is
used to correct unstable bits under noisy operating conditions, which enhances the robustness of
the proposed PUF. Experimental results show that the main performance and MA-resilience of
the proposed PUF are closer to the ideal value. The inter-die variations are not considered in this
article, and we will further purchase more FPGAs to further validate the efficacy of PUFs. In the
future, several PUF-based security solutions are explored to resist software and hardware attacks
and ensure the integrity and trustworthiness of embedded systems.
